# Phase 10 — Combined 3-Axis Quasi-Static Cases

**Estimate:** 1–2 focused days  
**Depends on:** Phase 09

## Outcome

Apply user-supplied longitudinal, lateral, and vertical accelerations as quasi-static inertial loads and solve the four contact loads and suspension state.

## Questions this phase answers

- What point-contact vertical loads are consistent with a given `(ax, ay, az)` case?
- How much body roll/pitch and bump-stop compression does the current elastic setup predict?
- At what acceleration does a contact lift or a travel limit engage?

## Tiny implementation modules

1. Acceleration-case schema with units, reference frame, gravity convention, and optional bank/grade held out for later.
2. CG inertial force and body moment assembly, with a declared elastic/geometric roll-moment assumption.
3. Continuation sweeps in longitudinal, lateral, vertical, or combined acceleration.
4. Contact active-set handling for zero normal load.
5. Equilibrium/free-body summary with forces, moments, attitudes, and residuals.

## Implementation notes

The initial model treats acceleration as an externally supplied quasi-static condition, not something generated by tires. Tire contacts provide vertical point reactions only. The solver may report the horizontal resultant needed to sustain the case, but it does not determine whether the tires can produce it.

Before suspension geometry supplies lateral force paths, the user must select one explicitly simplified roll-moment mode: all sprung roll moment carried elastically from a ground-level force path, or user-entered effective front/rear roll-axis heights and sprung/unsprung mass assumptions. A sensitivity sweep over plausible heights is preferable to one false-exact value. Phase 23 replaces these entered assumptions with force-path calculations where the geometry supports them.

Use `az = 0` for steady level-road acceleration where gravity remains a separate load. If telemetry provides accelerometer “specific force,” it must be converted before entering this model; Phase 13 owns that mapping.

The external vertical and moment equilibrium determines total, axle, and side load changes. Suspension compatibility and stiffness determine the remaining diagonal distribution and body attitude.

## Human review required

- Approve acceleration and gravity sign conventions using one braking and one left-corner example.
- Decide whether CG coordinates describe total or sprung mass; total mass is the only supported initial mode.
- Review lift-off behavior before using results near two-wheel support.

## Validation

- Zero acceleration reproduces Phase 09.
- Analytic rigid-body front/rear transfer `m ax h / wheelbase` and left/right transfer `m ay h / track` are recovered for equal tracks and centered CG.
- Mirroring lateral acceleration mirrors left/right loads.
- Total normal force responds correctly to prescribed vertical inertial acceleration.
- All solved force/moment residuals are exported.

## Exit criteria

Users can run a grid of combined G cases and inspect four loads, body state, component forces, stop engagement, lift-off, and declared assumptions.
