# Rigid travel stops and stroke dots

Top-out and bottom-out are exact unilateral constraints on corner travel. Both the initial and final head-to-head body solves enforce the geometry-derived wheel-travel boundaries. A stopped corner does not penetrate farther as its load grows: its stop reaction changes and the other body degrees of freedom can still move. The stop releases when equilibrium no longer needs a reaction in its supported direction. No large penalty rate or clipping of the final picture is used.

The three body coordinates are heave, roll and pitch. For each possible set of up to three independent active stop normals, the solver solves elastic equilibrium and contact equalities together, then checks all travel inequalities and reaction signs. Bump reactions are positive and rebound reactions negative in the suspension-force convention. The accepted reaction is included in normal loads and force/moment residuals, and is separate from finite elastic parallel-element forces. Four simultaneous rigid contacts have non-unique individual reactions; the minimum-norm nonnegative distribution preserves body equilibrium and symmetry.

An active stop has infinite one-sided loading stiffness. Front-only rigid engagement gives 100% front RSD, rear-only gives 0%; with both axles rigid, RSD and RCB are undefined and show **—**. Elastic spring/bar terms remain available separately. Reversing load can release a stop, so this infinite tangent is not a finite spring resisting motion in both directions. JSON exports and live state encode infinite stiffness as `"Infinity"` and undefined ratios as `"NaN"`.

Each spring-axis element has three circular dots: white upper and lower mounting points, plus a gold stroke marker (red at a travel boundary). Let U and L be its mounts, ℓ their current separation and ℓmin the fully compressed component length. The marker is:

`U + normalize(L − U) × (ℓ − ℓmin)`

Thus its distance from the upper mount is the remaining compression stroke in metres. At bottom-out it coincides exactly with that mount; the two mounting points themselves are never repositioned to fake a stop. At full rebound, the marker-to-upper distance equals the full configured stroke. The nearby B/R readouts show remaining component-axis bump/rebound stroke in mm; wheel jounce remains a separate readout. Micron-scale reconstruction tolerance closes the display marker at an exact solved boundary.

The editable front/rear component stroke remains in `data/synthetic/gr86/travel-limits.json`. The obsolete finite hard-stop rate has been removed. These stroke estimates still use the entered spring-axis geometry; independent rear damper mounts have not been measured. Existing synthetic geometry, centered-rack force equilibrium, one local-tangent update, and unsolved tire/contact lift remain the broader model limits. A steered inspection pose that would exceed actual component stroke is rejected instead of displayed through a rigid stop. The provisional stiffness update can still give non-monotonic responses across load cases; exact stop feasibility is checked independently of that approximation.

Validation: analytic heave and single-corner constraints, increasing load, unloading/release, progressive elastic curves, unilateral reaction signs, force/moment closure, a 24-direction 2G sweep, and all four stroke-marker contact positions. Historical free-travel baseline cases are retained. Historical finite-penalty stop results in `data/testdata/visualizer/baseline.json` are preserved as history and superseded at active stops. App-owned captures and the export/undefined-ratio proof are under `artifacts/hard-stops`; run the app with `--hard-stop-proof` to repeat that short visual check.
