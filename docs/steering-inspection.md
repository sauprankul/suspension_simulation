# Steering and alignment inspection

The steering-wheel slider/text input sits above the G controller. Positive input turns left. At the default ±450° input, both rack inner joints move together along body Y by ∓10 mm. This conservative travel estimate keeps the short synthetic front ARB endlinks reachable at static ride height. It is editable and is not an OEM steering-ratio measurement. Larger rack travel and extreme suspension positions can make a tie rod or endlink unreachable. Such inputs retain the last accepted A/B scene and mark it stale with a reason.

The shared geometry dialog edits dimensions in millimetres and angles in degrees. Data are stored in SI in `data/synthetic/gr86/steering.json`. Coordinates use +X forward, +Y left, +Z up, with origin at the static CG ground projection. Rack inner joints are center ± half-spacing along Y. Outer pickup offsets are relative to the static wheel center; the left Y offset is mirrored on the right. Neutral tie-rod lengths are derived from these endpoints and stay fixed as the rack moves. Changed rack/pickup geometry rebuilds the centered-rack travel maps and derived stiffness, and must remain reachable over the package's validated travel branch. Geometry that fails this validation is rejected before saving.

Front/rear static camber and total toe calibrate each installed wheel plane relative to its upright. They do not model the adjustment mechanism or move the suspension hard points. Positive camber is top outward; positive total toe is toe-in. Each wheel receives half the axle's static total toe, with opposite signs. The live camber and heading readings use the final world wheel orientation, including body attitude, upright motion and static alignment. Front left/right headings emerge from the rack and tie constraints, including their geometric Ackermann difference; no ideal Ackermann curve is imposed.

Axle total toe is right heading minus left heading. Thrust is the circular mean of the two headings. Per-wheel Δ bump toe compares body-relative heading at the current jounce with zero jounce at the same steering input, with positive meaning additional toe-in. Effective steering ratio is the absolute steering-wheel angle divided by front thrust; it is blank at zero thrust. Suspension-induced thrust affects that instantaneous ratio.

The force equilibrium and stiffness still use the centered rack. Steering is a constrained kinematic inspection at that accepted body equilibrium. Steering-induced spring/bar force changes, jacking, compliance and tire/contact equilibrium are not coupled back into that solve. Front bar endlinks and component stroke are checked against the steered upright pickups; impossible poses are rejected rather than stretched. Travel boundaries are now [rigid constraints](rigid-travel-stops.md), with a separately solved reaction and an infinite one-sided loading tangent.

The initial bump-steer diagnosis confirms that the synthetic hard points are responsible for the large effect: with zero rack movement, FL heading changes from 0° at static ride to about +8.54° at 50 mm rebound and −5.84° at 50 mm bump. Rack height and pickup edits change this curve. These numbers describe the synthetic package, not a measured GR86.

The 264-pixel dashboard keeps its size through steering, travel limits and rejected inputs. Longer diagnostics live behind **Details**. Labels identify modeled physical parts; schematic chassis connections and pivot-axis guides are omitted. White labels use separate screen cells and leader lines, and follow component-layer visibility. The cube orbits around a screen-plane axis perpendicular to a drag; its ring rolls around the viewing axis. Model dragging pans. These camera actions do not solve physics.

Validation: 78 regression checks; `--steering-proof` verifies both lock directions, stable normal/limit/stale layouts at 1280×720 and 1600×900, white component labels and camera independence. Reports and captures are under `artifacts/steering`. `kinematics.json` records reachable and unreachable rack/jounce samples. Live logs and exported snapshots include the requested steering input, configuration and accepted wheel/axle readings.
